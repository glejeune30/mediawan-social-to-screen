## 1. From Social to Screen (0:20) · Christine

CHRISTINE: Good afternoon. We are Team 21: I'm Christine, with Fiona, Gabin and Gabriel. In five
minutes, we will show you the problem we chose, the decision our radar helps Mediawan make, and a
first test on real adaptations.

## 2. By the time a story is mainstream, its rights are gone (0:45) · Christine

CHRISTINE: Stories are now born online: on BookTok, BookTube, Reddit, Webtoon or Wattpad, long
before they reach the charts. [Fans discover them, recommend them to each other, and build an
audience on their own.]

Three examples. Fourth Wing: only five months between the novel's release and Amazon developing
the series. Heartstopper: when Netflix ordered the series, there was not a single page about it on
English Wikipedia, so a mainstream tool would simply not have seen it. Solo Leveling: four years of
growing global fandom before the anime was announced.

So the window to option a story is short, and it opens in communities, not in charts. Fiona will
now explain the decision we want to help Mediawan make.

## 3. One question for Mediawan's development teams (0:45) · Fiona

FIONA: Thank you, Christine. Our radar answers one business question: which book or webtoon should
Mediawan's development teams look at, and option, this quarter, before its rights become
expensive?

Every week, each story gets one of three statuses. Watch: early buzz, we keep it on the list.
Prepare: it is accelerating and spreading, so someone reads it and checks who holds the rights.
Act now: strong growth while the mainstream is still unaware, so we contact the rights holder.

We focus on books and webtoons because Mediawan adapts both, in fiction and in animation, and
because an option is a concrete and costly decision. [The earlier you move, the cheaper the
option.]

## 4. Existing tools see topics, not stories (0:30) · Fiona

FIONA: Before building anything, we looked at what already exists. These tools see topics, not
stories. Google Trends sees a story once millions of people search for it. Exploding Topics and
Glimpse track keywords and products. TikTok Creative Center tracks hashtags, for advertisers.
Parrot Analytics measures demand for shows already on screen.

None of them gives one score per story, across communities, timed to the rights window. That is
our angle. Gabin will show you how it works.

## 5. Three questions, asked every week for every story (0:50) · Gabin

GABIN: Thank you, Fiona. Every week, for every story, the radar asks three questions.

One: is the buzz accelerating?
Two: is it spreading, across several communities?
Three: is the mainstream still unaware? We compare the niche buzz with Google searches and
Wikipedia.

The answers become a score from 0 to 100, and a status.

We listen where stories are born: YouTube reviewers, Reddit fan communities, and AniList and Open
Library, where fans and readers log what they read. Those two are already live in our prototype,
which tracks more than a hundred stories this week. And we compare them with a mainstream gauge:
Google Trends and Wikipedia. Public data only, official APIs, no personal data.

## 6. First test: 3 of 4 stories flagged before the deal (1:15) · Gabin, then Gabriel

GABIN: Does it work? We ran a first test with free search data only, on four real adaptations. The
rule was fixed before we looked at any result: the last three months of Google searches at least
double the six months before, two months in a row.

Coral is the radar alert, dark is the deal. Solo Leveling: 42 months early. A Good Girl's Guide to
Murder: 28 months. Fourth Wing: three and a half months. Heartstopper was missed: it grew tenfold,
but too slowly for the rule. Gabriel?

GABRIEL: Thank you, Gabin. We did not want to fool ourselves, so we also tested the opposite: 16
popular stories with no adaptation listed, chosen before looking at their curves. The rule fired
for 11 of the 12 that were truly never adapted. So acceleration alone catches the hits, but it is
not selective: it is a first filter, which is why the radar asks the two other questions. And a
bonus: four of those stories had in fact been optioned, including the Twisted series, bought by
Netflix in January 2025, about three years after our alert.

## 7. Next: from a test to a weekly watchlist (0:40) · Gabriel

GABRIEL: Before On the Grill 2, we will add YouTube and Reddit, combine the three questions to cut
false alarms and measure the result on the same control group, and tune our prototype, which
already builds a weekly watchlist from live public data.

To make it truly useful for you, we have three questions: which genres matter most to your labels
right now, how many months of warning your teams need to option a story, and whether you could
share one or two past deals, won or lost, that we could test against. [Your answers will directly
shape our next version.]

Thank you. We are happy to take your questions.

## 8. Backup: the prototype (only if the jury asks what the user sees) · Gabin or Gabriel

This is our prototype, running on live public data. Every week it tracks the books and webtoons
that are gaining readers and fans, gives each one a status and a score, and explains why in plain
words. You can filter by format, genre or status, and open a page for each story with its curves.
Everything you see was collected this week, without any paid tool.
